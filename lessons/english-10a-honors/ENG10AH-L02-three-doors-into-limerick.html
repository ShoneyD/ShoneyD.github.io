      Part 1 — Pick your door

      McCourt didn't invent Limerick. The dole, the damp rooms and the price of bread were all real. Other subjects (economics, history, public health, museum design) have their own ways of looking at those things. Today you borrow one of those ways, use it to look at the McCourts, make something with what you find, and show it.

      Story words, and old Irish money — open this any time
      SettingWhere and when, and what it feels like to be there: cold, damp, crowded, loud.
      CharacterWho someone is, shown by what they want, do and say.
      ConflictThe problem pushing the story. Person vs. person, person vs. society (like poverty), or person vs. self.
      DialogueWhat people say. McCourt-style: no quotation marks, so the words run straight into the memory. Mam said, There's no bread till Friday.
      Old Irish money12 pence (d) = 1 shilling (s). 20 shillings = 1 pound (£). So 17s 6d means 17 shillings and 6 pence.
      SourceWhere a fact came from. Name it well enough that someone else could find it again.

      Interdisciplinary means borrowing another subject's tools (math, history, public health, museum design) to see something in the story that reading alone doesn't show you.
