      Introduce the Concept

      Part 1 — Three kinds of witness

      Four words, before we start

      claim
      a sentence somebody could argue with
      refute
      to push against a claim — to say it is wrong
      thesis
      the one claim your whole essay is trying to prove
      source
      where a piece of evidence came from, and who it came from

      Every source you will ever use got what it knows in one of three ways. That is the whole system, and it is worth having in your head before you go looking for anything.

      Here is one done for you — the research question

      Did the 1938 radio broadcast really cause a mass panic?

      I'll read this out loud — just follow along.

      On the night of 30 October 1938, a radio play told listeners that Martians had landed in New Jersey. It was written to sound like a real news broadcast. The next morning, newspapers all over America reported that the country had panicked. People have been arguing ever since about how much of that panic actually happened.

      Three real sources argue about that question. Here they are, already sorted:

      Source 1 · from the moment itself (primary)

      The front page of a newspaper printed on 31 October 1938: "Radio Listeners in Panic, Taking War Drama as Fact."

      Written the next morning by people who were there. Pushes for: yes, there was a panic.

      Source 2 · someone who looked into it (secondary)

      Hadley Cantril, a Princeton researcher, interviewed listeners afterwards and published a book about it in 1940. He worked out that roughly one in six of the people listening were frightened.

      Looked into it later and reported what he found. Complicates it: frightened is not the same as a nation in panic.

      Source 3 · someone arguing about it (commentary)

      In 2013, two media researchers argued that the panic was mostly invented by newspapers, who wanted radio to look dangerous because radio was taking their advertising money.

      No new facts about that night — an argument about what the known facts mean. Pushes against: no, America did not panic.

      The source made at the time is the one that turns out to be least reliable here. Being there does not make you right — and that is why an essay needs more than one kind of source.

      Part 2 — Fact or claim?

      A thesis is not a true sentence. A thesis is a sentence somebody could argue back at. That is the whole test, and it is the one most people skip.

      If nobody can disagree with your sentence, you have written a fact — and you cannot write an essay defending a fact.

      Three sentences about that same night:

      Sentence A

      The broadcast aired on 30 October 1938.

      Sentence B

      Newspapers exaggerated the panic because radio was taking their advertising money.

      Sentence C

      About six million people were listening.

      Homework

      Part 1 — Pick your thesis

      This is the claim your essay will spend its whole life defending. Three to choose from. Pick one. All three work. Every one of them already has somebody waiting to argue back — that is what makes it a thesis and not a fact.

      Option A · 1938

      The War of the Worlds panic was mostly invented by newspapers, not caused by the broadcast.

      Somebody will answer: there are real reports of people fleeing their homes that night. You cannot wave those away.

      Option B · schools

      Middle and high schools should be required to start after 8:30 a.m., even when it costs the district money.

      Somebody will answer: bus routes, jobs and practice times are real constraints, not excuses.

      Option C · scams and security

      Every high school should be required to teach students how to spot a scam message.

      The three kinds, and the thesis test — open this any time
      From the moment itself
      (primary)      		 Made while it was happening, by someone who was there. Records, transcripts, footage, letters, the thing itself.
      Someone who looked into it
      (secondary)      		 Studied it afterwards and reports what they found. Studies, reports, textbooks, news write-ups.
      Someone arguing about it
      (commentary · in English, criticism)      		 Adds no new facts. Takes facts everyone has and argues about what they mean. Opinion essays, reviews, literary criticism.

      The thesis test: can somebody argue back at your sentence? If no — it is a fact, and you cannot write an essay defending it.

      Being an expert does not make a source primary. What decides it is the job the source is doing, never the job the author has.
