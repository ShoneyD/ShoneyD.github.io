      Introduce the Concept

      Part 1 — Us and them

      Last lesson's question was where's the monster? Today's is harder: who gets made into one?

      The Gothic has a habit. It takes a group of people a society is nervous about and turns them into the monster. That move has a name: othering.

      What happens. 1897. Count Dracula, a rich nobleman from Eastern Europe, buys a house in London and ships himself there. He feeds on English people and turns them into creatures like him. The heroes hunt him down and drive him out.

      Listen for: what he wants from London.

      I long to go through the crowded streets of your mighty London

      Q1. Victorian readers in London found this terrifying. What fear is the book playing on?

      Part 2 — It cuts both ways

      What happens. 1818. Victor Frankenstein builds a man from dead body parts and brings him to life. He is horrified by how the creature looks and runs away. Everyone the creature meets screams, hits him or chases him off. Alone, he teaches himself to read and speak. Only then does he turn violent.

      Listen for: who he blames.

      Everywhere I see bliss, from which I alone am irrevocably excluded.
      I am malicious because I am miserable.

      Everyone else gets to be happy, and I'm locked out forever. I'm cruel because I'm miserable.

      Part 1 — The house remembers

      Toni Morrison, Beloved (1987). Morrison was a Black American novelist, and she won the Nobel Prize in Literature.

      What happens. Ohio, 1873, after slavery has ended. Sethe escaped slavery years ago. When slave-catchers came to take her family back, she killed her baby daughter rather than let her be enslaved. Now her house, number 124, is haunted by that baby's angry ghost. Then a young woman named Beloved shows up at the door.

      Listen for: how the first line talks about the house.

      124 was spiteful. Full of a baby's venom.

      Part 2 — Pulled back in time

      Octavia Butler, Kindred (1979). Butler was a Black American writer, best known for science fiction.

      What happens. California, 1976. Dana is a Black writer, newly married. Without warning she gets pulled back in time to a plantation in Maryland in the early 1800s, again and again, to save the life of the white boy who will grow up to be her own ancestor. Every trip, she has to survive as an enslaved woman.

      Listen for: what it costs her.

      I lost an arm on my last trip home.

      Independent Mastery

      Part 1 — The polite monster

      Jordan Peele, Get Out (2017). A horror film, and one of the best-known Gothic stories of this century.

      What happens. Chris, a young Black photographer, goes with his white girlfriend Rose to meet her rich parents at their big country house. Everyone is extremely friendly. Too friendly. They keep praising him for being Black. The Black people who work at the house act strangely, like someone else is inside them. The truth: the family lures Black people there and sells their bodies to rich white buyers, who take them over.

      Who gets made the monster, and where it lives — open this any time
      OtheringMaking a group of people into 'them': strange, dangerous, not like 'us'. The Gothic does this all the time.
      It cuts both waysThe Gothic can feed the fear (the foreign invader) OR make you feel for the outsider (Frankenstein's creature).
      ClassRich vs. poor. Often the monster is the powerful one feeding on everyone else, like an aristocrat vampire.
      Where's the monster? (now three answers)OUTSIDE: something comes for you. INSIDE: a mind, a hunger. THE SYSTEM: a history, a family, a whole society.
      Black women writersToni Morrison (Beloved, 1987) and Octavia Butler (Kindred, 1979). The ghost and the time travel are real, and the real horror is slavery.
      21st centuryGet Out (2017). The monster smiles, says the right things, and still treats people as property.
