      Part 3 — The -ing train

      Sometimes we don't mean every day. We mean right now, while it's happening. For that, English uses an -ing verb plus a helper. Remember the alone test? Shooting can't be the whole verb by itself. It needs a helper, and the helper carries the time.

      The three times, the -ing train, and the traps — open this any time
      ⏪ Simple pastAlready happened: shot, practiced, won. Clues: yesterday, last week, ago.
      ⏺ Simple presentHappens regularly, or is true now: shoots, practices. Clues: every day, these days, every Saturday.
      ⏩ Simple futurewill + plain verb: will shoot. Clues: tomorrow, someday, next week, in ten years.
      The -ing train (progressive)Going on at that moment. The helper carries the time: was/were + -ing · am/is/are + -ing · will be + -ing.
      Helper trapAfter will, did, didn't, does, the main verb stays plain: will win, didn't go, Did she see it?
      Rule-breakersshoot → shot · win → won · catch → caught · throw → threw · take → took · go → went · see → saw · beat → beat
      One story, one timePick a time and stay there. A verb that jumps out is a time-jumper. Exception: words in quotation marks keep the time the speaker said them in.
      Run-onA new subject + verb starts with no period. Fix: a period, or , and / , but / , so.

      Same word, different job still counts: was is linking in Lena was nervous (Lena = nervous) and helping in Lena was aiming (it leans on aiming).
