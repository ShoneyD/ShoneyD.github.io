      Part 2 — Does it carry across?

      Trans means across, like transport. A transitive verb carries its action across to something: an object. An intransitive verb doesn't carry anything. The action stops right there.

      Watch the middle column. That question does all the work.

      Q4. "After practice, Maya packed her arrows into the case." Type only the direct object.

      Q5. "Lena ran home." Is home a direct object?

      My answer

      Check my answer — after you've written yours

      No. Home looks like a noun, but ask ran what? Nothing answers. Home answers where, like to the field. So ran is intransitive here.

      Carry across or stop, the What? test, and fake objects — open this any time
      Transitive (carries across)The action carries across to an object. Lena grabbed her bow. Without the object, the sentence is broken.
      Intransitive (stops)The action stops at the verb. Lena laughed. Nothing needs to come after.
      The What? testSay the verb + what? (or whom?). If the sentence answers, that answer is the direct object.
      Fake objectsPhrases that answer where, when or how: to the field, at noon, home, very fast. They are never objects.
      Double agentsSome verbs work both ways: Lena won the medal / Lena won. Lena ran a race / Lena ran fast.
      Linking verbs (L07)was, seemed, looked: what follows describes the subject (= test). It's not an object.
      Run-on (L07–L08)A new subject + verb with no period. Fix: a period, or , and / , but / , so.

      Trans means across, as in transport: the action is carried across to something.
