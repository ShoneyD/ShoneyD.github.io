    Introduce the Concept

    Part 1 — Letters as mouth pictures

    Use your own mouth for these, not the chart. Say the sound out loud first, then look at the shape.

    Most alphabets happened by accident, over centuries. Korean didn't. In 1443 King Sejong sat down and designed one, on purpose, so that an ordinary person could learn to read in a morning instead of spending years on Chinese characters. The handbook that came with it explains the reasoning letter by letter — which is why you don't have to memorise these. You can work most of them out from your own mouth.

    Five consonants. Say each sound out loud before you look at the shape.

    Letter Name Sound What the shape is a picture of
    ㄱ giyeok g / k The back of your tongue humping up to block the roof of your mouth.
    ㄴ nieun n Your tongue tip pressing the ridge behind your top teeth.
    ㅁ mieum m Your closed lips, seen from the front.
    ㅅ siot s A tooth. The air hisses through the gap.
    ㅇ ieung silent An open, round throat with nothing blocking it.

    The one weird one

    Every Korean syllable has to start with a consonant. So when a syllable really begins with a vowel sound, ㅇ sits in the front seat and stays silent. It's holding the spot, not making a noise.

    Six vowels, built from three pieces. A standing person ㅣ, the flat earth ㅡ, and the sun — originally a dot, now a short stroke.

    Letter Sounds like How it's built
    ㅣ ee, as in see The standing person, on their own.
    ㅡ eu — no English version The flat earth, on its own. Say "ee" but with flat, spread lips.
    ㅏ ah, as in father Person, sun on the right.
    ㅓ uh, as in duh Person, sun on the left. Lips relaxed — not rounded.
    ㅗ oh, as in go Earth, sun above it. Lips pushed forward and round.
    ㅜ oo, as in moon Earth, sun below it.

    Q1. Say "mmm" and then "nnn" slowly, and pay attention to where in your mouth each one happens. Now look at ㅁ and ㄴ. What is each shape showing you?

    Q2. ㅗ and ㅜ are made of exactly the same two pieces — the sun just moves. Which is "oh" and which is "oo", and what's the rule that tells you?

    Part 2 — Read three blocks

    Say each one out loud first, then write how it sounds. Take the block apart: what's the consonant, and what's the vowel?

    고
    너
    스
    Check my answers — after you've written all three

    고 = go. ㄱ plus ㅗ. Flat vowel, so it sits underneath.

    너 = neo, said like "nuh". ㄴ plus ㅓ. If you wrote no, you're close but you've slipped into ㅗ — ㅓ has relaxed, unrounded lips. It means "you", by the way.

    스 = seu. ㅅ plus ㅡ. This is the vowel with no English version — "ee" with flat, spread lips. If you wrote su or soo, you've landed on ㅜ instead.
