      Part 2 — One letter, three ways

      English has two settings for most consonants. Korean has three, and the third one is the reason Korean has letters that look like they are stuttering.

      Take ㄱ. Add one stroke and you get ㅋ. Write it twice and you get ㄲ. Three letters, one mouth position, and the only thing that changes is what the air does.

      Every family works the same way, and there are only four of them, plus one that never got a third member:

      Q2. 파 and 빠. One of them makes the paper jump and one leaves it still. Which is which, and how do you know from looking at the letters alone?

      Sentence starters — use them if you want

      • 파 is the one that ___, because ____.

      You don't have to use these.

      My answer

      Check my answer — after you've written yours

      파 makes the paper jump — it is ㅂ with a stroke added, and an added stroke always means more air. 빠 is ㅂ written twice, and doubled always means no air at all. You never have to remember which is which: the shape tells you.

      The three-way families, the four joined vowels, and the phrase bank — open this any time
      ㄱ → ㅋ → ㄲg → k with a puff → tight k, no air · 가 · 카 · 까
      ㄷ → ㅌ → ㄸd → t with a puff → tight t, no air · 다 · 타 · 따
      ㅂ → ㅍ → ㅃb → p with a puff → tight p, no air · 바 · 파 · 빠
      ㅈ → ㅊ → ㅉj → ch with a puff → tight ch, no air · 자 · 차 · 짜
      ㅅ → ㅆs → tight s · 사 · 싸. This family has only two — ㅅ never got an aspirated partner.
      ㅘ ㅝ ㅚ ㅟㅗ+ㅏ = ㅘ (wa) · ㅜ+ㅓ = ㅝ (wo) · ㅗ+ㅣ = ㅚ (we) · ㅜ+ㅣ = ㅟ (wi)
      Phrase bank네 yes · 아니요 no · 저기요 excuse me · 커피 마셔요 I drink coffee · 노래해요 I sing · 바빠요 I'm busy · 짜요 it's salty · 싸요 it's cheap · 커요 it's big · 아파요 it hurts · 최고 the best
      Two freebies있어요 there is / I have · 없어요 there isn't / I don't have. Copy these. Do not try to work out why they are spelled that way — that is next week, and it is the whole lesson.

      The paper test. Hold a strip of paper in front of your mouth. Plain moves it a little. Added stroke makes it jump. Doubled does not move it at all.

      Everything in the phrase bank is a whole sentence on its own. Korean does not need the I — 바빠요 by itself already means I'm busy.
