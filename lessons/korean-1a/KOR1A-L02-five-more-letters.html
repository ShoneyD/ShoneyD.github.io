      Q2. A block can never be a vowel sitting by itself. In your own words — what is the circle in 오 actually for, and what would go wrong without it?

      My answer

      Check my answer — after you've written yours

      A Korean syllable block is a little box with seats in it, and the front seat has to have a consonant in it. When the sound starts with a vowel there is no consonant to put there, so ㅇ sits in the seat and stays silent. Without it you'd have a loose vowel with nowhere to live — and Korean simply doesn't write those.

      If you said something like “it holds the space” or “it's a placeholder”, you have it exactly. If you said “it makes an ‘o' sound” — close, but that's the vowel ㅗ doing that, not the circle.

      The rule that catches everyone: a vowel can never sit in a block by itself. If a syllable starts with a vowel sound, ㅇ takes the front seat and says nothing. Tall vowels (ㅏ ㅓ ㅣ) go to the right of the consonant; flat vowels (ㅗ ㅜ ㅡ) go underneath it.
