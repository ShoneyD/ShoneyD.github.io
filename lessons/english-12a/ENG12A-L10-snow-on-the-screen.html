      Introduce the Concept

      Part 1 — The three-link chain

      A writer can tell you how to feel. A good writer shows you with a comparison, and lets the feeling do the work. Every answer today follows the same three links:

      Here is the same snowfall, written two ways. Nothing about the snow changes, only what it is compared to. Read both out loud if you like, then answer Q1 and Q2.

      Line A
      The snow settled over the town like a quilt, tucking every roof in for the night.      		 Line B
      The snow settled over the town like a sheet pulled over a face.

      Q1. What tone does Line A give off?

      My answer

      Q2. What tone does Line B give off?

      My answer

      Now the third link. If a whole story were full of Line B images, what might the story be saying about winter, or about being alone? One sentence is plenty.

      Q3. Swapping the comparison changed the tone. What does that do to the idea the story could be about?

      Words for today — open this any time
      Figurative languageSaying something that isn't literally true so the reader sees it a new way. The snow isn't really a blanket.
      SimileA comparison using like or as: the room was quiet as a library.
      MetaphorA comparison that says one thing IS another, no like or as: the hallway was a tunnel.
      PersonificationGiving a non-human thing a human action or feeling: the wind complained all night.
      ToneThe writer's attitude, the feeling the words give off: lonely, tender, tense, playful, eerie.
      ThemeWhat the story is saying about life. A full sentence, not one word: 'Love can travel even when people can't.'

      The chain: comparison → feeling (tone) → idea (theme). A good answer touches all three links.
