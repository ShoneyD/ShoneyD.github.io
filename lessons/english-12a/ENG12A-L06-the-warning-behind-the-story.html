      Introduce the Concept

      Part 1 — Why he wrote it

      A writer doesn't just tell you things. Why they're writing decides what they put in, and that decides what you walk away understanding.

      Try it on an ad. A road-safety ad shows a phone buzzing on a car seat, in slow motion, right before a crash. No one speaks.

      There's more than one good answer. Most readers land near: when a government controls what people see, say and remember, people lose their freedom, even inside their own heads.       The words for today — open this any time
      Author's purposeWHY the writer wrote it. What they want you to think, feel or do afterwards.
      ThemeThe big idea about life the text keeps pointing at. A full sentence, not one word.
      SourceAnywhere information comes from: the book itself, facts about the writer's life, the writer's own words.
      EvidenceA detail or quote, plus which source it came from, plus what it shows.
      TotalitarianA government that controls everything: the news, the past, even what people are allowed to think.
      PropagandaInformation made to push people into believing what those in power want.

      The chain: purpose → choices → what the reader understands. Every answer today is one link of it.
