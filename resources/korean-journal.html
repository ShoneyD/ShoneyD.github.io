The Korean Journal

Ten minutes. One page. Every day.

Why on paper. Every Korean word you have written so far, you built by clicking blocks that were already made. That proves you can recognise a syllable. It does not prove you can make one — and making one is the thing a keyboard will never teach you. Your hand has to learn the shape. So this one is a pen and a notebook, and it is the fastest ten minutes in the week.

The page

The four moves

1

Copy five · about 2 minutes

Five words from the bank, Korean and English, in your own handwriting. Say each one out loud as you write it — not before, not after. Sound and hand at the same time is the whole trick.

Start with any word carrying dots from earlier pages. Those are the ones your brain has flagged.

2

Read five out loud · about 2 minutes

Cover the English with your hand. Read the five Korean words aloud. Any word that did not come to you instantly gets a small dot beside it. Not wrong — just slow. Slow is the thing worth catching.

Instantly means instantly. If you had to work it out letter by letter, that is a dot, even though you got it right.

3

Write three from sound · about 3 minutes

Three words you are not looking at. Pick them from yesterday's page, or have someone say them to you. Hear the sounds, build the blocks, write it. Then check.

This is the only move that can actually go wrong, and that is the point of it — the other three you can do half asleep. Getting one wrong here is worth more than getting five right in move 1.

4

One line of your own · about 3 minutes

Anything, as long as you chose it and it is in Hangul. A name. Two words that sound good together. A lyric you can spell. A list of the things in your room you have letters for. It does not have to be correct Korean and it does not have to be a sentence.

Stuck? Take a line straight out of the phrase bank below and put one English word beside it — that counts, and it is how most days will look at first. Or write the name of something you watched or listened to this week; there is almost always one you can spell.

The dot system

A dot means that one was slow. Dots are the only record you keep and they run the whole thing.

Three dots on one word → it goes to the front of tomorrow's copy-five, and it stays there until it stops collecting them.

No dots for a week → that word is done. Stop writing it.

Done looks like

One dated page. Five words copied, five read aloud, three written without looking, one line that was your idea. If it took more than about twelve minutes, you did too much — cut move 1 to three words, not move 4.

Once a week

On the last page of the week, take the five words with the most dots and write them from sound alone, with the notebook closed on every other page. That is the only test in the whole system, you set it yourself, and it is over in two minutes.

Then take one photo of the week's pages and upload it to the journal assignment in Canvas. It is graded on pages done, not on whether the Korean is right — a page with mistakes counts exactly as much as a clean one. Thirty seconds, once, on Sunday.

Bring to the lesson

The notebook, open at the week's pages. The first two minutes of class are the dotted words, out loud.
