The Tell

Five lines from “The Tell-Tale Heart.” The narrator keeps telling you why he did it. His tone keeps telling you something else. Listen to the tone.

Part 1 — Hear the tone

Tone is the attitude in the words — how he sounds, not what he says. Read each line (out loud helps), then tap the tone you hear. You can change your mind before you press Check.

The four tones

Part 2 — What he really wants

He tells you his reason himself: the old man’s eye. Don’t take his word for it. His tone gives away what he is really after — and that is his motivation.

Pick the real reason behind each line. Three of the reasons belong to none of the lines — and one of those three is the reason he gives you himself.

Part 3 — What the story is saying

A theme is what the story is saying about people in general — not just about this one man. His motives are how Poe delivers it: we watch one person want something, and learn something true about anyone who wants it.

Tap the big idea each line points at.

More than one line can point at the same idea. One of these five is genuinely arguable — if you can back it, you're right.

Part 4 — Your turn

Now build one. Invent a character — someone in a game, a rider at a horse show, a kid who just broke something, anyone. They tell us one reason for what they did. Their tone gives away the real one.

Starters — only if you want one
