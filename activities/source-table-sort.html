The Source Table

One question, six sources, two sides. Work out how each source got what it knows — then what it does to the question.

How this works

  1. Drag a card into a panel — or click the card, then click the panel. Both work.
  2. Press Check it. Wrong ones come back to you with a hint. You get as many tries as you want.
  3. Nothing is timed. At the end, one button copies everything for Canvas.

Part 2 — What does it do to the question?

Naming the kind of source is the easy half. The other half is what it does to the question — does it push for the claim, or against it, and how hard? Drag the right job into each box. Two of the five cards are decoys — they don't belong anywhere.

Part 3 — Say it yourself

One question. You can type it or say it out loud and I'll type — your call, and don't worry about spelling.

Q1. You are writing the essay that says schools should start later. Of the six sources you just sorted, which one would you actually put in it? Name it, say what kind of source it is, and say why that one is the one you would trust.

My answer

Sentence starters — use them, that's what they're for

• The source I would use is ____.
• It is a ____ source, because ____.
• I would trust it because ____.

Done looks like

Three things in your answer: which source, what kind it is, and why you trust it. Two or three sentences is plenty.
