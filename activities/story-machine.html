Plan a short story, turn in the plan, then write it one part at a time with the plan right above every box. At the end it all merges into one story you copy into Canvas.

Step 1: Choose your literary form

Tap one. Under each name is what a reader expects from that kind of story. Your story will have to show those things, so pick one that sounds fun to write. Any of them can get a full grade.

“Other” needs your teacher’s OK. Ask before you go on.

Step 2: Your main character

Short answers. A few words in each box is plenty. You are collecting ideas, not writing sentences yet.

Example from a different story (don't copy it)

Marisol, 15, the second-fastest swimmer on her team. Wants: her relay spot back. Struggles with: she won't ask for help. Detail: chews her goggle strap. Talks: short sentences, jokes when she's nervous.

Spelling doesn't count in the plan. You can say any answer out loud and your teacher will type it.

Step 3: Who else is in the story?

Your main character needs at least one person to talk to, or argue with. That is where your dialogue comes from. A second extra character is optional.

Example

Coach Reyes, Marisol's coach. Wants: her to rest her shoulder. Talks: calm, answers a question with a question.

Three things a reader will sense there

Step 5: What's the problem?

Tap the kind of conflict your story has. Stories can have more than one; pick the main one.

Q1. The conflict in one sentence. The first part is already filled in from Step 2, so finish the blank.

Q2. The conflict has to change or challenge your main character. How are they different by the end?

How they change

Example

Marisol wants her relay spot back, but her shoulder isn't healing and she won't tell anyone. At the start, Marisol hides everything. By the end, Marisol asks for help out loud.

Step 6: Plan the plot, five parts

Write one event per line, a few words each. These lines become your checklist when you write, so keep them short. Each dot on the arc turns green when its part is planned.

Step 7: The message, and where your form shows up

Theme, in one minute

  • A theme is a whole sentence about life, not one word. “Friendship” is a topic. “Real friends tell you what you don't want to hear” is a theme.
  • You plan it here, but you never write it into the story. The events should make a reader feel it on their own.
