The Swap Test

Six short passages. Each one has an attitude, and that's its tone. Find the tone, find the one word making it, then run an experiment: swap that word and see if the whole tone flips.

How this works

  1. Part 1: name the tone (4 min). Part 2: find the word (4 min). Part 3: the swap test (5 min). Part 4: your own sentence (3 min).
  2. Finish one part, then press the green button when you're ready for the next.
  3. Retry as many times as you like. Nothing is timed and nothing is scored.
  4. At the end, one button copies everything for your Canvas submission.

Part 1 — Name the tone

Tone is the writer's attitude: how the words sound, not what happens. Two people can ride the same hill, and one sounds thrilled while the other sounds scared. Read each passage (out loud helps), then tap the tone you hear.

Done looks like: a tone tapped on all six passages, then Check.

The four tones

Part 2 — Find the word

Writers build tone out of word choice, also called diction. In each passage, one word is doing most of the work. Most of the other words would sound the same in any mood.

Tap the word that makes the tone. Trick: imagine the word disappearing. If the feeling goes with it, that's your word.

Done looks like: one word tapped on all six, then Check.

Part 3 — The swap test

Time for an experiment. Everything in the passage stays the same except one word. Each passage has a target tone. Tap a swap word and the passage re-reads with it, so try as many as you want.

Your hypothesis, before you start

If you change just one word, what will happen to the tone?

Watch out: some swaps keep the same tone, and some make the passage go flat, with no tone at all. Only one hits the target.

Done looks like: every passage hitting its target tone, then Check.

Part 4 — Your turn

Write one sentence about something you actually do: riding, swimming, building, a trip, an experiment, anything. Pick a tone and make one word carry it. Then run the swap test on your own sentence.

Done looks like: one sentence with the tone word in CAPITALS, plus one swap word and the tone it flips to. You can say it out loud instead of typing, and your teacher will type it.

Starters, only if you want one

• My sentence sounds ____ because of the word ____.
• If I swap ____ for ____, it turns ____.

Use one, or ignore both and write it your way. Spelling doesn't count here.

Take this with you

Next time you read anything, a book, a text, a video caption, try the one-word question: which word is making it sound this way? Then ask what would happen if it were a different word.
