Part 1 — Where the sound is made

Korean letters were designed, not inherited. In 1443 each consonant was drawn as a little picture of what your mouth does to make that sound.

Say each sound out loud first, and notice where it happens. Then click a letter, then click the description that matches it.

Part 2 — Two strokes and a dot

Every basic vowel is built from three pieces: ㅣ a standing person, ㅡ the flat earth, and a short stroke that started life as a dot — the sun.

Where the sun sits decides the vowel. Work out where it goes.

Part 3 — Put the name on the board

Korean doesn't run letters in a line. Each syllable gets packed into its own square block, and the blocks sit side by side.

Click a consonant, then a vowel. Watch what happens.
