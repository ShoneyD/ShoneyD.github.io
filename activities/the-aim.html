The four moves

Part 2 — What it does to you

Naming the move is the easy half. This is the half that matters: what does it actually do to the person reading it?

Pick the effect that belongs to each line. Two of the effects belong to none of them — leave those unused.

Part 3 — Aim it at something bigger

Every one of these lines is about something small and specific — one job, one holiday, one afternoon, one day in a girl's life. And every one of them is aimed at something much bigger that has nothing to do with that specific thing.

That's the trick of good nonfiction: the small true thing is the delivery system. Tap the bigger idea each line is aimed at.

More than one line can be aimed at the same idea. One of these five is genuinely arguable — if you can back it, you're right.

Part 4 — Your turn

Now you do what they did. Pick any of the four moves and any bigger idea — one of ours, or one of your own that isn't on the list.

Then write a few lines of your own nonfiction: something small and true that you could aim at it. A queue you stood in. A room in your house. Something a stranger said. It doesn't have to be important — Orwell's is about a job, Hurston's is about a porch.

Starters — only if you want one

• The word ____ is doing the work, because it makes the reader ____.
• It's about ____, but it's aimed at ____.
• I used the ____ because I wanted you to feel ____ before I said it.

Pick whichever you'd rather — or ignore all three and write it your way. That's the better answer if you've got one.
